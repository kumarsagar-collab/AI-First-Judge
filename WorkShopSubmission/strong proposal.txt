Sample Proposal A: Evidence-Based AI Service Assistant

Table T01 • Contoso Service Operations AI Modernization

POSITIVE CONTROL SAMPLE • Expected to score well, with identifiable gaps

1. Executive recommendation

Contoso should deliver a bounded 12-week MVP that assists service agents with intake summarization, grounded troubleshooting guidance, and draft work-order preparation. Approved equipment manuals remain authoritative; resolved work orders provide contextual evidence only. Human approval remains mandatory for priority, safety actions, customer commitments, and financial entitlement. The MVP is designed to validate measurable value before broader regional rollout.

2. Customer outcome and success hypothesis

Area | Proposal

Customer outcome | Reduce avoidable manual effort and improve consistency of service intake and work-order preparation without weakening safety, control, or supportability.

Baseline plan | During discovery, sample current handling time, search time, rework, citation availability, and escalation rate across selected service categories.

MVP measures | Median time to a usable draft; percentage of recommendations with valid citations; human acceptance or correction rate; escaped quality issues; cost per processed case; safety and compliance exceptions.

Decision gate | Expand only if agreed quality, safety, adoption, and unit-economics thresholds are met.

3. Options considered

Option | Assessment | Decision

Option 1: Search enhancement | Improve manual search and templates only. Lowest change risk, but limited automation and slower value realization. | Use as fallback if source readiness is low.

Option 2: Assisted AI workflow | Grounded summarization and recommendation drafting with human approval. Balanced value, control, and delivery feasibility. | Recommended for the MVP.

Option 3: Autonomous orchestration | Agent independently classifies and updates work orders. Higher potential automation, but unacceptable for the initial safety and governance posture. | Defer until evidence and controls justify it.

4. Recommended solution and architecture decisions

Intake layer normalizes requests from approved channels and removes unsupported instructions before processing.

Grounding layer retrieves from approved equipment manuals and selected support articles; historical work orders are clearly labeled as contextual.

Assistant generates a structured case summary, possible troubleshooting steps, citations, confidence indicators, and unresolved questions.

Human approval gate is required before consequential updates or customer commitments.

Integration writes only approved fields to the work-order system through least-privilege access.

Observability records tool calls, retrieval sources, latency, errors, evaluation outcomes, and user corrections.

Fallback presents retrieved sources and a manual workflow when confidence is low, sources conflict, or a dependency is unavailable.

5. Delivery plan

Phase | Activities | Exit evidence

Phase 0: Validate | Weeks 1-2: confirm priority use cases, source authority, access model, baselines, risks, and evaluation set. | Approved scope; curated source list; baseline; threat and RAI review; MVP backlog.

Phase 1: MVP build | Weeks 3-7: implement intake, retrieval, structured response, approval, telemetry, and test automation. | End-to-end demonstration; traceable citations; critical security tests pass.

Phase 2: Pilot readiness | Weeks 8-10: user testing, red-team cases, operational procedures, cost tuning, and defect remediation. | Quality and safety thresholds met; support ownership accepted.

Phase 3: Controlled pilot | Weeks 11-12: limited cohort rollout, monitor outcomes, and make scale recommendation. | Go, revise, or stop decision supported by evidence.

6. Cost model and optimization

Estimated first-phase delivery cost: USD 340,000 to USD 410,000, within the scenario ceiling. This is a planning range, not a quote.

Cost category | Range | Primary sensitivity

Engineering and integration | USD 180k-215k | Varies with work-order API maturity and access approvals.

Knowledge preparation | USD 55k-70k | Varies with source quality, metadata, and conflict resolution.

Evaluation, security, and readiness | USD 60k-75k | Includes representative test sets, red-team cases, operational controls, and handoff.

Contingency | USD 45k-50k | Applied to integration and regional unknowns.

Optimization choices: narrow MVP scope, reuse approved platform capabilities, route simple tasks to the least costly adequate method, constrain context, avoid unnecessary model calls, cache stable retrieval where permitted, measure cost per case, and defer autonomous actions. Savings are not claimed until Contoso provides volume and baseline operating-cost evidence.

7. Security, privacy, and Responsible AI

Use approved enterprise services, least privilege, user-context access enforcement, and data minimization.

Do not grant unrestricted mailbox, chat, or historical-record access.

Make AI use visible and provide citations, confidence, and easy correction or override.

Test normal, edge, conflicting-source, prompt-injection, permission, dependency-failure, and safety scenarios.

Define owners for knowledge freshness, prompts, evaluations, incidents, access reviews, and operating cost.

Require human approval for priority, safety, customer commitment, and entitlement decisions.

8. Risks, assumptions, and decisions required

Risk / assumption | Why it matters | Treatment

Source inconsistency | Manuals may conflict with historical cases. | Apply source hierarchy; show conflict; route to human review.

Integration complexity | Work-order APIs and field rules are not yet confirmed. | Validate in Phase 0; retain manual fallback.

Volume uncertainty | Case volumes and peak demand are unknown. | Benchmark during discovery and model cost sensitivities.

Adoption risk | Agents may distrust or over-trust recommendations. | Use transparent evidence, training, feedback, and acceptance monitoring.

Decisions requested from Contoso: approve the initial use-case boundary; nominate authoritative content owners; provide integration and baseline data; confirm pilot users and acceptance thresholds.

9. AI-first working method

The team will use approved AI tools to accelerate scenario analysis, backlog drafting, test-case generation, documentation, and code assistance. Every material output will be reviewed by the accountable consultant, architect, or delivery lead. The team will retain a log of AI-assisted artifacts, validation performed, rejected recommendations, and reusable assets created.

Known gaps for the judge to identify

The proposal does not provide a detailed target architecture diagram, named Azure services, customer-specific volume model, regional data-residency decision, or final acceptance thresholds. These remain discovery items and should prevent a perfect score.
