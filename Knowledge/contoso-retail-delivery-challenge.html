Step 1 · Get aligned

Understand the Project Objectives

Welcome to Sprint 0. Before the larger team arrives, your mission is to turn a signed contract into a shared, executable understanding of the customer outcome. Get curious, challenge assumptions, and make the next two weeks count.

About Contoso Retail

Contoso Retail is a fictional omnichannel retailer operating stores and digital channels. Microsoft recently signed an AI implementation contract, and your table is the core team starting Sprint 0. A larger delivery team joins in two weeks and must be able to hit the ground running.

Work as one project team

Divide the work across the table. Have one or two participants act as customer Business/Product Owners. Challenge assumptions, make decisions together, and keep a concise decision log as one cross-functional project team.

Two-week Sprint 0 objective

Establish sufficient clarity on outcomes, scope, requirements, customer decisions, prototype direction, delivery work, governance, risks, dependencies, acceptance, and adoption so the larger project team can hit the ground running.

Shared Statement of Work summary

Purpose: Validate and prepare one assigned AI-enabled retail business capability for implementation, creating an agreed foundation for the larger delivery team.

Sprint 0 duration

Two weeks immediately before the larger delivery team joins.

Customer responsibilities

  • Provide Product Owner availability and timely decisions.
  • Share current-process examples, policies, and known exceptions.
  • Provide sample content and descriptions of relevant data.
  • Review and accept Sprint 0 artifacts within the working cadence.

In-scope activities

  • Outcome and scope alignment, customer workshops, and rapid prototype creation.
  • Requirement validation, BRD development, and a prioritized delivery backlog.
  • Estimation assumptions and a high-level project, release, and sprint plan.
  • Governance and ways of working, including decisions, reviews, and escalation.
  • Risk, assumption, issue, and dependency identification.
  • Responsible-AI considerations, operational readiness, and a SteerCo readout.

Expected deliverables

  • Validated prototype and workshop decision record.
  • Business Requirements Document.
  • Prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria.
  • High-level delivery plan and estimation assumptions.
  • RAID summary, governance, and ways of working.
  • SteerCo presentation.

Acceptance criteria

  • Business owner confirms the prototype direction.
  • Requirements are complete enough to trace and govern.
  • The backlog supports credible delivery planning.
  • Dependencies and assumptions are visible.
  • SteerCo can make the required decisions.

Out of scope

Production deployment, full-scale integration, migration, operational rollout, and final production acceptance. Contract value, named systems or vendors, detailed dates beyond the two-week Sprint 0, and committed benefits are intentionally not provided.

Sprint 0 finish line

  • Outcome and scope aligned
  • Prototype validated
  • BRD and backlog ready
  • Dependencies and RAID visible
  • Plan agreed
  • SteerCo decisions prepared

Scenario 01

On-Shelf Availability and Store Replenishment

Executive sponsor: Chief Operating Officer / Head of Store Operations

Store operations Inventory priorities Associate action Customer availability

Business context / problem

Fictional Contoso Retail sees uneven product availability across stores. Teams must balance stockouts, excess inventory, local demand signals, promotional activity, delivery timing, and limited associate capacity. Replenishment choices are not consistently translated into clear, timely store actions.

Primary personas / users

Store manager: approves exceptions, transfers, and orders while protecting the morning workload. Department lead: reviews priorities and assigns shelf actions. Inventory planner: balances availability, promotion, delivery, and excess exposure across stores. Store associate: checks the backroom, completes shelf work, and records exceptions. Regional operations leader: reviews outcomes and recurring barriers.

Relevant products, channels, processes, or journeys

High-velocity and seasonal assortments; store shelves and pickup availability; stock review, replenishment prioritization, backroom-to-shelf action, transfer or order decisions, exception handling, and outcome review.

Clear business objective

Prepare a capability that helps operations teams identify and act on the most important availability and replenishment priorities while balancing customer need, inventory exposure, and store workload.

BCurrent process today

  1. Central inventory planner reviews prior-day sales, stock position, promotions, and delivery plans, then sends store-level priorities. Friction: the list is broad and does not reflect each store's opening workload.
  2. Store inventory lead compares the central list with the morning inventory report. Friction: shelf, backroom, and incoming stock can show different or stale quantities.
  3. Department lead performs spot checks and asks associates to inspect the backroom. Friction: urgent promotional gaps and genuine customer needs compete without a shared priority rule.
  4. Store associate searches the backroom and records whether stock is found. Friction: location notes are inconsistent and repeated searches consume limited action time.
  5. Department lead assigns shelf fill, display correction, substitution signage, or count-check tasks. Friction: associates receive more work than they can finish before peak trading.
  6. Store associate completes the shelf action or records an exception such as no stock, damaged units, blocked access, or incorrect shelf capacity. Friction: deferred work and reasons are not consistently visible.
  7. Store manager and regional operations leader approve transfers or orders, review unresolved exceptions, and compare outcomes. Friction: learning arrives after the next planning cycle and recurring causes are difficult to see.

CWhere the customer intends to use AI

Contoso Retail intends to use AI to identify likely shelf gaps, prioritize the actions that matter most, explain the business drivers, and propose alternatives when stock is unavailable. It should learn from completed, deferred, and unsuccessful actions so future priorities become more useful. Associates confirm what happened; department leads adjust tasking; and store managers retain approval for policy exceptions, transfers, and orders. The business decision being improved is which limited store actions to take first to protect availability without creating excess or unmanageable workload.

DPrototype v1 must demonstrate

  • Morning store worklist — department lead: store, category, priority, product, shelf/backroom quantity, promotion, reason, due time, owner, and assign/defer actions.
  • Product and store detail — inventory lead: recent sales, shelf capacity, current counts, delivery window, open tasks, promotion, and neighboring-store availability.
  • Priority explanation — department lead: visible drivers, information freshness, uncertainty, affected customer commitment, and a way to contest the priority.
  • Recommended alternatives — store manager: fill from backroom, correct count, substitute display, request transfer, place order, or defer, with consequences shown.
  • Action confirmation and exception — store associate: found quantity, completed quantity, result, exception reason, note, and request for help.
  • Outcome view — store manager: completed, deferred, unresolved, availability result, workload used, repeat issues, and approvals awaiting decision.

FBusiness rules and exceptions to show

  • Essential items and known pickup commitments take precedence when customer impact is otherwise equal.
  • Promotional items influence priority but do not automatically outrank essential availability or confirmed customer commitments.
  • Do not assign more than 14 morning actions per pilot store without a store leader explicitly deferring other work.
  • Transfers and orders require store-manager approval; cross-region transfers also require inventory-planner review.
  • If quantity information is older than two hours, contradictory, or missing, show uncertainty and require a physical check before an exception decision.
  • Priorities may support work decisions but must not be used alone to evaluate associate performance; people can contest, correct, or explain an outcome.

Scenario 02

Personalized Omnichannel Product Discovery

Executive sponsor: Chief Marketing Officer / Chief Digital Officer

Product discovery Loyalty context Digital-store continuity Customer consent

Business context / problem

Customers encounter broad, inconsistent discovery experiences across digital and store journeys. Contoso Retail wants greater relevance while respecting customer choice, consent, availability, merchandising commitments, and the difference between helpful personalization and intrusive treatment.

Primary personas / users

Anonymous visitor: wants useful discovery without identifying themselves. Loyalty customer: wants relevant choices and continuity under clear consent. Gift shopper: needs guided ideas without recommendations being mistaken for their own preferences. Store associate: assists only with customer permission and needs current product context. Merchandising and privacy owners: protect assortment integrity, fair exposure, customer choice, and policy compliance.

Relevant products, channels, processes, or journeys

Search, browse, comparison, inspiration, store visit preparation, associate-assisted discovery, saved preferences, loyalty interactions, cart or list continuity, and post-purchase discovery across product categories.

Clear business objective

Prepare a consent-aware product discovery capability that improves relevance and continuity across digital and store journeys while preserving customer control, merchandising integrity, and fair treatment.

BCurrent process today

  1. Customer enters a phrase, browses a category, or follows a campaign link. Friction: vague intent and everyday language often map poorly to catalog terms.
  2. Customer applies filters for price, features, brand, and fulfillment. Friction: filters differ by category and can remove useful alternatives without explanation.
  3. Customer opens several products and manually compares details. Friction: important differences, suitability, and diverse choices are difficult to scan.
  4. Customer checks store or delivery availability. Friction: relevance can look good until an item is unavailable in the selected location.
  5. Customer saves a list or cart. Friction: anonymous sessions, devices, and consent choices can break continuity.
  6. Customer visits a store and decides whether to share the saved list. Friction: permission is unclear and gift-shopping activity may be mistaken for personal preference.
  7. Store associate searches again, checks availability, and suggests alternatives. Friction: the associate may not know the customer's constraints or why digital items were selected.
  8. Customer and business owner complete, abandon, or provide feedback; teams later review aggregate outcomes. Friction: dislike, irrelevance, unavailable items, and privacy concerns are not captured consistently.

CWhere the customer intends to use AI

Contoso Retail intends to use AI to understand stated shopping intent, suggest relevant and varied products, explain why each option may fit, and preserve continuity when a customer chooses to carry a list into a store visit. A useful non-personalized path must always remain available, and continuity must follow the customer's consent and preferences. The customer chooses products, controls sharing, corrects preferences, and can reject or reset suggestions; merchandising and privacy owners set the boundaries. The outcome being improved is confident product discovery with less effort, better availability alignment, and no loss of customer control.

DPrototype v1 must demonstrate

  • Discovery entry and choice — customer: intent phrase, category, budget, must-have needs, selected location, and clear personalized or non-personalized choice.
  • Recommendations — customer: product image placeholder, name, price, availability, key fit, “why shown,” variety label, save, dismiss, and refine actions.
  • Comparison — customer: three products with differences in features, suitability, price, availability, and a neutral explanation of trade-offs.
  • Saved-list continuity — loyalty customer or guest: list name, items, notes, availability changes, channel history, and controls to continue, duplicate, or clear.
  • Associate handoff — customer and associate: explicit share permission, expiry, shared constraints, approved list content, associate alternatives, and stop-sharing action.
  • Feedback and control — customer: more/less like this, not relevant, unavailable, gift shopping, manage preferences, reset history, and return to non-personalized discovery.

FBusiness rules and exceptions to show

  • Personalization requires an explicit, current customer choice; loyalty membership alone is not blanket permission.
  • The non-personalized path must remain complete, findable, and usable for anonymous and opted-out customers.
  • Recommendations must show availability for the selected store or area and clearly label uncertain or changing availability.

  • Gift-shopping and one-off research can be marked so those actions do not silently alter enduring preferences.
  • Associate access requires customer permission with a visible scope and expiry; sensitive preferences are excluded, and the customer can stop sharing at any time.

Scenario 03

Customer Care and Order Exception Resolution

Executive sponsor: Chief Customer Officer

Delayed orders Substitutions Refunds and returns Resolution quality

Business context / problem

Customers contact Contoso Retail when orders are delayed, items are substituted, refunds are unclear, or return status is uncertain. Contact-center and store-service teams must navigate fragmented policies, incomplete context, ownership boundaries, and escalation paths while protecting customer trust.

Primary personas / users

Customer: wants a clear answer and fair remedy without repeating the story. Contact-center advisor: investigates, explains options, and decides within authority. Store-service associate: resolves pickup issues using store context. Supervisor or finance reviewer: approves higher-value or uncertain remedies. Policy owner and customer-experience leader: monitor consistency, customer effort, and unresolved patterns.

Relevant products, channels, processes, or journeys

Delivery and pickup orders; delayed or missing items; substitutions; cancellation; refund and return status; contact center, messaging, and store service; handoff, escalation, recovery, and follow-up.

Clear business objective

Prepare a capability that enables faster, more consistent, policy-aligned order exception resolution while improving advisor productivity, customer understanding, resolution quality, and accountable escalation.

BCurrent process today

  1. Customer and advisor or store associate begin by phone, message, or store visit; the service colleague verifies identity and locates the order. Friction: customers repeat details and order context differs by channel.
  2. Advisor classifies the issue and asks for missing facts, photographs, pickup details, or preferred contact method. Friction: classification is inconsistent and missing information is often discovered late.
  3. Advisor searches policies for delivery, pickup, substitution, refund, or return-status eligibility. Friction: multiple pages can conflict or omit disruption exceptions.
  4. Advisor, store, or fulfillment colleague investigates the timeline, item status, payment, prior contacts, and ownership. Friction: handoffs and incomplete status create uncertainty.
  5. Advisor compares permitted remedies such as replacement, partial refund, full refund, collection, wait-and-monitor, or goodwill. Friction: consequences and authority limits are not visible together.
  6. Advisor, supervisor, or finance reviewer makes or approves the remedy and records the rationale. Friction: pending approvals leave the customer without a clear expectation.
  7. Advisor explains confirmed facts, unknowns, remedy, timing, and next steps in the customer's preferred channel. Friction: messages can overstate uncertain delivery or refund timing.
  8. Case owner monitors completion, follows up, and closes or reopens the case. Friction: ownership, promised dates, and repeat contact are not consistently tracked.

CWhere the customer intends to use AI

Contoso Retail intends to use AI to summarize the customer's case, identify missing information, present policy-aligned options, draft a clear customer explanation, and flag uncertainty or escalation needs. Advisors verify the facts and make decisions within their authority; supervisors and finance reviewers approve higher-value or exceptional remedies. The outcome being improved is a faster, more consistent resolution that distinguishes confirmed facts from estimates and gives the customer a clear owner, remedy, and next step.

DPrototype v1 must demonstrate

  • Case queue — advisor or store associate: case ID, age, channel, issue, customer impact, order value, priority reason, owner, approval state, and accept/assign action.
  • Consolidated case and timeline — advisor: customer preference, order/items, fulfillment events, payments, prior contacts, promises, known facts, unknowns, and missing-information request.
  • Policy and eligibility guidance — advisor: applicable rule, effective date, eligibility result, evidence used, uncertainty, exception path, and acknowledgment.
  • Remedy and approval — advisor, supervisor, or finance reviewer: available options, amount, authority level, customer consequence, rationale, approve, reject, revise, or escalate.
  • Customer message preview — advisor: channel, tone, confirmed facts, estimates, remedy, timing, accessibility preference, edit, and send-for-review action.
  • Resolution tracking — case owner and leader: promised date, next action, owner, pending dependency, customer response, reopened status, and outcome measures.

FBusiness rules and exceptions to show
